# Solo Practitioner Pain Cohorts Attacked by AI Products

## Scope

This document identifies the main solo-practitioner pains attacked by AI assistant products in professional mental-health practice.

The focus is not generic CRM, ordinary admin automation, or autonomous therapy. The relevant product battlefield is the conversion of clinical work into usable documentation, continuity, treatment planning, measurement, billing-support evidence, and regulated workflows.

## 7 Pain Cohorts

| # | Pain cohort | What the pain is | Products attacking it |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Progress-note production is unpaid cognitive labor | After sessions, practitioners must reconstruct what happened, decide what matters, and write a usable clinical note. | Mentalyc, Upheal, SimplePractice Note Taker, TherapyNotes TherapyFuel, TheraNest, TherapyAppointment, Owl, JotPsych, Valant |
| 2 | Notes must fit accepted clinical formats | Clinical notes must fit recognizable documentation structures such as SOAP, DAP, BIRP, GIRP, PIE, intake, progress, supervision, discharge, or custom templates. | Mentalyc, Valant, TherapyNotes, Owl, TherapyAppointment |
| 3 | Treatment plans are hard to keep linked to actual sessions | Practitioners need to maintain the clinical golden thread: problem → goal → intervention → client response → progress → next plan. | Blueprint, Mentalyc, JotPsych, TherapyNotes, Valant, TherapyAppointment |
| 4 | Session prep and case continuity are weak | Before a session, practitioners need fast recall of themes, risks, goals, prior interventions, homework, unresolved issues, and likely next moves. | Blueprint, SimplePractice Care Aide, TherapyNotes, TheraNest, Mentalyc |
| 5 | Outcomes and measures are scattered or underused | Measures such as PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, assessments, worksheets, and longitudinal progress signals often sit outside the practitioner’s active clinical reasoning loop. | Blueprint, JotPsych, Valant, Oasys |
| 6 | Insurance, audit, and reimbursement defensibility is stressful | Documentation must support reimbursement, coding, audit defense, medical necessity, and denial prevention. | JotPsych, Valant, Eleos, TherapyNotes |
| 7 | Using AI with client data creates vendor-risk work | Solo practitioners must evaluate whether AI tools are safe enough for PHI/client data: BAA/DPA, retention, deletion, sub-processors, no-training policy, access control, and audit posture. | Upheal, SimplePractice, TherapyAppointment, Owl, Eleos, Valant |

## CRM / Practice Management Boundary

Ordinary admin is substantially addressed by CRM, Practice Management, and EHR systems:

- scheduling;
- reminders;
- payments;
- client forms;
- client portal;
- basic client records;
- simple task and follow-up tracking.

AI assistant products are attacking the remaining clinical-workflow gap: session material does not automatically become a defensible clinical record, continuity system, treatment-plan update, measurement signal, or reimbursement-support artifact.

## Product Battlefield

AI assistant products for solo practitioners primarily sell:

1. less unpaid clinical documentation work;
2. more acceptable and structured clinical notes;
3. better continuity between sessions;
4. treatment-plan linkage;
5. more usable outcome and measure tracking;
6. better reimbursement and audit defensibility;
7. lower friction using AI inside a regulated practice.

## Compressed Positioning Line

Solo practitioners do not mainly need another CRM. They need the clinical residue of every session — notes, continuity, plans, measures, and defensibility — to stop leaking into unpaid cognitive labor.
