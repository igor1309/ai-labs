# Apple platforms and a third-party proximity payment

For the detailed evidence on BLE without opening the app, see [Apple BLE without opening the app](apple-background-ble.md). Foreground-only is a deliberate v0.0.1 scope choice. The later-version suggestions below are product proposals, not platform requirements or verified limits.

This note answers what an ordinary third-party iOS app can do for a Mizan-style phone-to-till flow. “Payment” below means the product flow unless explicitly described as a card-network payment: moving a confirmation and result over BLE does not itself authorize, clear, or settle money. Apple’s APIs and entitlements describe platform capability, not a guarantee that Apple will approve a particular product or entitlement request.

## 1. Core Bluetooth in the background

An app that needs BLE callbacks after it leaves the foreground declares `bluetooth-central`, `bluetooth-peripheral`, or both in `UIBackgroundModes`. The system can wake a suspended app for relevant Bluetooth events. This is event-driven background execution, not a continuously running service. Without those modes, an app suspended in the background cannot scan or advertise.

Background peripheral advertising is constrained: the local name is omitted, and advertised service UUIDs move to an overflow area that only an iOS device explicitly scanning for those UUIDs can discover. Advertising is best effort even in the foreground; it is not a general-purpose broadcast channel. So the client must scan for Mizan’s service UUID, and the host cannot rely on a displayed name or arbitrary advertisement payload to identify a till.

Background scanning is also throttled. Duplicate discovery events are coalesced and `CBCentralManagerScanOptionAllowDuplicatesKey` is ignored; when all scanning apps are backgrounded, the scan interval increases. This removes the repeated RSSI samples the current nearest-host selector expects. Foreground scanning is the appropriate mode for selecting among nearby tills and making the user confirm the amount.

Core Bluetooth state preservation/restoration is opt-in through a manager restoration identifier and restoration delegate. It preserves configured scans, connections, subscriptions, published GATT data, advertising, and subscribed centrals, and can relaunch an app to handle a pending Bluetooth event. It does not promise a fresh app launch just because a nearby device exists. Apple’s current TN3115 adds a major iOS/iPadOS 26 qualification: after user force-quit, its force-quit entry carries an AccessorySetupKit qualification for apps that set up Bluetooth accessories. TN3115 attaches that qualification to particular table entries; it does not mark system memory removal or crash with that footnote. A generic Mizan peer-to-peer payment has not been shown eligible for the accessory exception.

**For the host and client:** the `bluetooth-central`/`bluetooth-peripheral` modes can help sustain an already configured interaction while backgrounded, but they do not turn the exchange into a dependable unattended payment. Keep both apps visible through discovery, amount review, payer confirmation, and result for v0.1.0 unless a tested iOS-version/device matrix proves a narrower background requirement. A state-restored connection must still revalidate session, amount, timeout, and user intent.

Sources: [Core Bluetooth background processing guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html), [Core Bluetooth advertising](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth/cbperipheralmanager/startadvertising%28_%3A%29), [Core Bluetooth framework overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth/), [TN3115: Bluetooth State Restoration app relaunch rules](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3115-bluetooth-state-restoration-app-relaunch-rules), [WWDC25: Finish tasks in the background](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/).

## 2. What can wake or open the app?

“App closed” has different meanings. A system-suspended or system-terminated app may be woken for specific events. A user force-quit is an explicit stop and blocks many relaunch mechanisms. No wake mechanism should silently start or complete a purchase; user-visible payment intent and confirmation remain required.

| Mechanism | Can it reach an app that isn’t running? | What it does for Mizan |
|---|---|---|
| **iBeacon region monitoring** | Yes, for a monitored entry/exit, Core Location may launch the app in the background (subject to authorization, hardware, Background App Refresh, and system conditions). iOS permits only 20 monitored regions per app; beacon ranging can refine proximity after wake. It is not a precise till selector. | Can trigger a contextual notification or prepare state. It cannot put the app’s payment screen in front of the user or guarantee timely discovery of the till’s live BLE transaction. Beacon monitoring is passive and approximate; use the foreground BLE scan for actual till selection. A normal iOS device acting as the beacon must stay foregrounded. |
| **Notification** | An alert can be shown while the app is closed; tapping it opens/foregrounds the app. A user-selected notification action may run app code in the background. A silent push may wake the app briefly, but delivery is low priority, throttled, and not guaranteed. | A reminder or a user-tapped “Continue” entry point. It cannot silently present payment UI or be treated as a guaranteed arrival trigger. |
| **Live Activity** | A Live Activity can show current activity while the app is not visible; tapping it opens the app, and interactive buttons can perform supported actions. It does not itself cold-start the app into payment UI. As of iOS 18.4, a Live Activity can let an app continue Nearby Interaction ranging in background with supported devices. Core Bluetooth documents expanded background privileges in iOS 26 when a `CBManager` exists and the app starts a Live Activity before backgrounding. | Useful to keep an already initiated session visible and, on eligible OS versions, aid an in-progress background session. It is not the trigger that discovers a new till and starts a transaction from a closed app. Live Activities are available on iPhone and iPad, but availability is user-controlled and system-limited. |
| **App Clip Code / NFC tag / QR** | A person invokes the App Clip by scanning/tapping a code/tag or tapping an invocation link. The system presents the App Clip card and launches the clip after the person chooses it; if the full app is installed, that app handles the invocation. | The strongest “wake” alternative for a till: a static App Clip Code or NFC-integrated code identifies the merchant/till and opens the experience. This is explicit user action and typically includes a visible launch step; then the foreground app can start BLE or hand off to a backend. It is not an invisible app wake, and an arbitrary NFC tag is not an App Clip invocation unless configured as one. |
| **App Intents / Shortcuts** | Intents can run in the background, in an extension, or bring the app forward depending on the intent and system context. Open intents are explicitly foreground-launching; many other actions run quietly. | A shortcut can open Mizan or perform a safe setup/status action. It cannot use a background shortcut to bypass the user’s payment review or create a reliable till-arrival trigger. A foreground intent can be a user-selected entry point, not an unattended purchase start. |
| **Wallet pass** | A pass can appear on the Lock Screen based on time/location and can support NFC presentation when provisioned and certified for an eligible contactless pass use case. A relevant pass notification can be tapped. | A loyalty/member pass can identify the merchant, but Wallet passes do not grant a third-party app arbitrary NFC tag emulation or BLE background scanning. NFC contactless Wallet passes require Apple’s NFC certificate/entitlement and compatible VAS-certified terminal/POS. It is not a general payment-app wake mechanism. |

## 3. NFC on the iPhone: reading, emulation, and merchant acceptance

**Reading tags:** Core NFC supports reading NFC tags and ISO 7816/NDEF data in a user-started reader session on compatible iPhones. This is not a background, always-on detector for arbitrary shop tags, and it does not expose the phone as a generic payment card. A till can use an App Clip Code/NFC tag as an explicit invocation, or a QR code; after launch the app can use BLE.

**Card emulation (HCE):** Apple’s `CardSession` supports ISO 7816 host card emulation only for approved in-store payments and other named credential categories. The EEA HCE route requires iPhone XS or later, iOS 17.4 or later, eligible EEA distribution/use, and Apple’s managed `com.apple.developer.nfc.hce` and AID-prefix entitlements. The developer must apply and satisfy Apple’s security, privacy, regulatory, and commercial terms; it is not arbitrary NFC tag emulation. Apple’s staff developer-forum answer explicitly says unlisted use cases are unsupported.

**The later-region NFC & SE route:** From iOS 18.1, Apple’s NFC & SE Platform supports specified in-store payments and credentials in the territories and iOS versions listed on Apple’s live page. At the date of this note, the listed iOS 18.1+ markets are Australia, Brazil, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, UK, and US (with later OS minimums for additional territories). This route uses the Secure Element and Secure Enclave, requires Apple agreements, Apple Business Register onboarding, an approved managed entitlement and product configuration, and (for payments) an appropriately licensed financial institution or PSP relationship. It is a possible regulated contactless-payment route, not an API for exchanging a custom Mizan BLE token at an ordinary terminal.

**Tap to Pay on iPhone:** This is the merchant/acquirer side: `ProximityReader` lets a merchant app on iPhone XS or later read a customer’s contactless payment method with no external reader. It requires Apple’s Tap to Pay entitlement, merchant terms acceptance, a supported country/region, and integration with a participating payment service provider that supplies certified terminal configuration and processes the transaction. It is iPhone-only; an iPad host cannot serve as the Tap to Pay reader.

Sources: [Core NFC](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corenfc), [CardSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corenfc/cardsession), [Apple HCE availability and terms](https://developer.apple.com/support/hce-transactions-in-apps/), [Apple developer forum: arbitrary emulation isn’t supported](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/762339), [NFC & SE Platform availability and requirements](https://developer.apple.com/support/nfc-se-platform), [Tap to Pay setup](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/proximityreader/setting-up-the-entitlement-for-tap-to-pay-on-iphone), [Tap to Pay integration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/proximityreader/adding-support-for-tap-to-pay-on-iphone-to-your-app), [supported regions and PSPs](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/regions/).

## 4. Apple Pay and why BLE can’t imitate it

In a contactless Apple Pay transaction, the terminal communicates with a payment applet in the device’s Secure Element over NFC. The Secure Element holds a device-specific Device Account Number (token), not the underlying card number. For a transaction it produces a cryptogram/dynamic security code using a transaction counter and key (and NFC terminal data such as the unpredictable number); the payment network/issuer validates it. User authentication and the Secure Enclave authorize access to the payment credential. Apple’s Platform Security guide describes this trust boundary and transaction path.

For app or web checkout, Apple Pay APIs return a payment token to the merchant’s processor; that is a distinct flow from presenting the iPhone as a contactless card to a terminal. A regular third-party app has no entitlement to read Apple Pay’s Secure Element credentials or to emit the same terminal-facing NFC protocol. The NFC & SE Platform, EEA HCE, and Tap to Pay are separately scoped, approved programs. BLE can carry Mizan’s own authenticated session messages between two Mizan apps, but it cannot turn those messages into an Apple Pay card-present transaction or replace an acquirer, network, issuer, terminal, and their security approvals.

**Implication:** v0.0.1/v0.1.0 can demonstrate proximity, amount presentation, consent, and a mocked result. A real payment requires integrating a licensed payment provider/backend with an authorized payment method. If the goal becomes accepting an existing contactless bank card at the host, evaluate Tap to Pay on an eligible **iPhone**; if the goal is Mizan itself as a contactless credential, evaluate the relevant NFC & SE/HCE program and business eligibility separately.

Sources: [Apple Platform Security: Apple Pay](https://help.apple.com/pdf/security/en_US/apple-platform-security-guide.pdf), [Apple Pay token format](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/payment-token-format-reference), [NFC & SE Platform](https://developer.apple.com/support/nfc-se-platform), [Tap to Pay](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/proximityreader/adding-support-for-tap-to-pay-on-iphone-to-your-app).

## 5. What differs on iPadOS for the host?

Core Bluetooth central/peripheral roles and the `bluetooth-central`/`bluetooth-peripheral` modes are available to iPad apps; Apple notes these background modes are not supported for iPad apps running on macOS. The same background advertising, scan coalescing and restoration opt-in apply; TN3115 qualifies particular relaunch cases with AccessorySetupKit in iOS/iPadOS 26. iPadOS does not provide the host with extra always-on execution simply because it is the till screen. Multitasking, locking, and suspension still matter, so the PoC should keep the Host screen active during an exchange and should restore a transaction only as an expired/validating state, never assume a completed payment.

For hardware paths, iPad is not eligible for Tap to Pay on iPhone and does not provide the iPhone contactless card-presentation route discussed above. Treat the host iPad as the BLE peripheral/display, not as a bank-card terminal. Live Activities are supported on iPad and may help display an already-started activity, but they are user/system-controlled and don’t supply a new transaction trigger.

Sources: [Core Bluetooth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth/), [TN3115](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3115-bluetooth-state-restoration-app-relaunch-rules), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities), [Tap to Pay device requirements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/proximityreader/paymentcardreader/issupported).

## 6. Nearby Interaction (UWB) and other ways to identify the till

Nearby Interaction can report distance and direction between compatible UWB devices (for example supported iPhones) or a supported accessory. The apps must discover each other and exchange discovery tokens/configuration over a separate data link such as BLE. It improves pointing/ranging after discovery; it is not a till discovery or payment transport API. Foreground ranging is the ordinary case. Background ranging is limited to Bluetooth LE-paired and connected devices, with a documented iOS 18.4+ Live Activity exception for supported devices. It also requires user permission and compatible hardware.

For Mizan, the existing BLE service UUID plus foreground RSSI selection is the simplest v0.x approach. RSSI only gives a noisy relative signal estimate; UWB can be a later experiment if supported-device coverage and a need for directional guidance justify it. Other options include an App Clip Code/NFC tag or QR code encoding a signed till identifier, followed by BLE/backend verification. Geofencing and iBeacon are too coarse to distinguish adjacent tills; an iPad’s iBeacon advertising must run in the foreground.

Sources: [Nearby Interaction overview and background requirements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/), [session discovery and token exchange](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/initiating-and-maintaining-a-session), [iBeacon on iOS devices](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/turning-an-ios-device-into-an-ibeacon-device), [App Clip launch options](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appclip/configuring-the-launch-experience-of-your-app-clip).

## What this allows for v0.1.0 and v0.2.0

- **v0.1.0 (drop explicit handshake):** keep both apps foregrounded; scan the Mizan service UUID, choose a host using current RSSI/window rules, establish the per-session authenticated channel, display the amount, require an explicit payer decision, and show a mocked result. Use background modes only if needed to preserve an in-progress GATT session, with timeout/recovery behavior tested on real devices. Do not promise “tap and pay while the app is closed.”
- **v0.2.0 (drop the foreground app):** Apple does not provide a general-purpose BLE wake-to-payment path. The practical product path is an explicit App Clip Code/NFC/QR invocation that opens a focused, foreground payment experience; optionally a Live Activity can keep an already-started session visible. A Wallet pass can identify a loyalty/member credential, not replace this flow. If “no separate reader” means accepting bank cards, evaluate Tap to Pay on iPhone with a participating PSP and use an iPhone as the merchant device. If Mizan itself must be presented contactlessly, the NFC & SE/HCE approval and eligibility path is a separate, substantial product and compliance track. Real money additionally requires a licensed PSP/payment service and backend authorization/settlement; BLE alone only transports the PoC protocol.
