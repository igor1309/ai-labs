# Payments over BLE and proximity: research notes

Research snapshot: 27 October 2026. This is a source map, not a security audit. Repository activity and licensing were checked from public project pages where available; recheck before adopting code.

## Findings and recommended reading order

1. **DOLE (Java Card offline P2P ledger)** — closest open implementation to a trustable local-value model. Read its architecture and transport limitations first. It keeps private keys, balances, monotonic counters and receive state on Java Cards; peers verify signed ledger transitions. BLE is an offline synchronization path on Android/Windows, not itself a payment guarantee. Its README says iOS lacks the needed extended advertising API. Thesis research prototype; **license not confirmed from project landing page in this pass**. [Repository](https://github.com/dominikkipfer/DOLE).
2. **Bank of England, Digital pound offline experiment (2025)** — best implementation-oriented comparison of BLE/NFC and secure-element tradeoffs. It reports a feasibility experiment, not real payments; local funds and secure elements address double spend/counterfeiting with residual risk and storage/performance limits. [Report](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2025/digital-pound-experiment-report-offline-payments).
3. **BIS Project Polaris handbook (2023)** — broad threat, architecture, operating model, privacy and risk-management survey. Use it to frame product decisions and threat model. [Handbook](https://www.bis.org/publications/project-polaris-handbook-offline-payments-cbdc).
4. **BeyondNet** — well-documented Android BLE store-and-forward demo. It carries signed/encrypted payment requests through relays but requires an online gateway and bank for final settlement; it is not offline finality. Good transport, queue, custody-ack and idempotency patterns. **No license shown on repository landing page**; demo/simulated INR, not production UPI. [Repository](https://github.com/Shikharyadav25/BeyondNet).
5. **Bolt Card / LNURL-withdraw** — useful proximity interaction and replay-protection pattern, but NFC card-to-reader plus a reachable service and Lightning node, not offline phone-to-phone BLE. Server authenticates the NTAG 424 DNA UID/counter and enforces increasing counters. MIT, but upstream explicitly says it is no longer maintained and points to Bolt Card Hub Phoenix Edition. [Repository](https://github.com/boltcard/boltcard), [specification](https://github.com/boltcard/boltcard/blob/main/docs/SPEC.md).
6. **BLE-specific attack literature** — BlueMirror (2021) on pairing/provisioning reflection and MitM flaws; BLERP (NDSS 2026) on re-pairing impersonation, downgrade and MitM, tested across Apple, Android and NimBLE. These make clear that “paired/encrypted BLE” is not enough without authenticated, bound application transactions. [BlueMirror](https://doi.org/10.1109/SPW53761.2021.00054), [BLERP](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/blerp-ble-re-pairing-attacks-and-defenses/).

## Published product and pilot designs

- **Digital euro offline pilot (ECB, current pilot materials):** offline P2P transactions are described as proximity NFC transfers; funding/defunding requires connection, while received offline funds may be re-spent. Pilot uses secure elements for protected local value. This is a pilot architecture, not a general public production service. [ECB pilot FAQ](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/pilot/html/ecb.faq-digital-euro-pilot.ga.html).
- **Digital pound experiment (Bank of England, 2025):** compares four prototype approaches and both NFC and BLE. Demonstrated technical feasibility and immediate finality based on locally held funds. Secure elements addressed double spend/counterfeit risk; capacity, speed and UX tradeoffs remained. [Experiment report](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2025/digital-pound-experiment-report-offline-payments), [design note](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2025/offline-payments-design-note).
- **Thailand retail CBDC pilot (Bank of Thailand):** fully offline design used physical stored-value smartcards and phone-mediated BLE/NFC to transfer value; report says hundreds of transactions could be made before synchronization. Relevant BLE+NFC architecture, but value is held by cards rather than ordinary phone app state. [Conclusion report](https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/documents/en/financial-innovation/cbdc-digital-currency/Update_Release_ENrCBDC%20Conclusion%20Report.pdf).
- **e-CNY “touch” and hardware wallets:** public pilot reporting described touching devices, including offline tests; primary technical detail is limited. Official Bank of China notes describe NFC SIM hardware wallets and NFC touch payments, Android-only in the cited launch. Do not assume BLE. [BOC SIM wallet announcement](https://www.boc.cn/aboutboc/bi1/202307/t20230710_23369110.html), [HKMA e-CNY overview](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/smart-consumers/e-cny/), [reported Suzhou touch trial](https://www.theblock.co/news/macro/2020-12-15-china-digital-yuan-touching-pay-87754).
- **Apple and Google mainstream wallet products:** public engineering/product documentation for Tap to Pay and Google Wallet describes NFC/EMV contactless acceptance, secure-element/tokenization and backend settlement. These are useful references for protected credential presentation and issuer/acquirer flow, not phone-to-phone BLE or offline stored-value settlement. [Apple Tap to Pay security](https://help.apple.com/pdf/security/en_US/apple-platform-security-guide.pdf), [Apple NFC & SE platform](https://developer.apple.com/support/nfc-se-platform/), [Google transit payment architecture](https://developers.google.com/wallet/tickets/open-loop/setup/technical-architecture). No directly applicable Apple/Google engineering talk describing BLE P2P stored-value payment found in this pass.
- **BIS Project Polaris:** frames offline functionality as system-level policy, hardware, privacy, operations and risk problem, with no universal solution. [Project page and reports](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc/polaris.htm).

## Security papers and references

- **Pairing / MitM:** [BlueMirror, IEEE SPW 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/SPW53761.2021.00054) reports reflection attacks in BLE/Classic pairing and provisioning, including mesh provisioning, with impersonation/MitM impacts under tested conditions.
- **Re-pairing downgrade / impersonation:** [BLERP, NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/blerp-ble-re-pairing-attacks-and-defenses/) analyzes standard BLE re-pairing and reports unauthenticated re-pairing/security downgrades, plus impersonation and MitM; authors evaluated 22 targets including Apple, Android and NimBLE.
- **Offline CBDC security survey:** [Review of Offline Payment Function of CBDC Considering Security Requirements (2022)](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/9/4488) covers counters, replay/double-spend, certificates, local logs and synchronization.
- **Offline payment protocol:** [Secure and Privacy-preserving CBDC Offline (IACR ePrint 2024/1746)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1746) explains why offline recipients cannot centrally check prior spend; combines secure elements with post hoc identification/deterrence.
- **Contactless relay:** [Relay Cost Bounding for Contactless EMV Payments (FC 2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-47854-7_11) and [ISO 7816-4 NFC relay countermeasure study (IEEE Access 2020)](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3031979). Range/proximity alone does not authenticate intent or prove physical distance; BLE’s longer range makes that assumption weaker.
- **System-level risk:** [Federal Reserve, A Robust Risk Framework for Offline Payments (2025)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/a-robust-risk-framework-for-offline-payments.htm) proposes consumer-smartphone offline payments using cryptography and tamper-evident secure elements.

## Implications for Mizan BLE beyond a mocked payment

A BLE exchange can prove that two nearby app instances exchanged bytes. It does not prove that money moved, that the counterpart is who the UI claims, or that the devices are physically close. Before treating a demo as payment-capable, Mizan needs explicit answers and controls for:

1. **Meaning and finality:** label mock, queued, authorized, settled, failed and reversed states separately. If no online authority is available, choose an explicit model (local stored value/ledger, signed deferred claim, or merely simulated interaction); do not call a deferred request “paid.”
2. **Identity and peer binding:** authenticate both roles and bind the transaction to payer/payee keys, role, amount, currency/asset, nonce, protocol version and expiry. Display the peer and amount for human confirmation. Bind acknowledgements to this exact transaction.
3. **Replay, duplication and concurrency:** unique transaction IDs/nonces, monotonic counters or equivalent durable state, idempotent retries, and duplicate/conflicting transaction handling across reconnects and app restarts. A BLE link-layer session alone does not supply application-level replay safety.
4. **Relay and proximity claims:** design for an attacker forwarding traffic between distant devices. BLE RSSI, scan/connect range and pairing do not establish distance; avoid “tap means physically present” security assumptions absent a separately justified distance-bounding mechanism or user-verified out-of-band intent.
5. **Pairing and session security:** use authenticated key agreement, verify peer identity and roles, reject unauthenticated re-pairing/security downgrade, derive fresh session keys, provide integrity/confidentiality, and avoid trusting cached bond state as transaction authorization.
6. **Offline double-spend and rollback:** if value is spendable offline, decide where authoritative balance/counter state lives and how cloning, rooted-device modification, snapshot rollback, key extraction and concurrent spends are prevented or bounded. Ordinary app storage and signatures alone cannot stop a key owner from signing conflicting spends.
7. **Interrupted/torn exchanges:** persist transaction state before acknowledging; make every step recoverable/idempotent; reconcile payer/payee state after app kill, radio loss or power loss. Define who bears a debit-without-credit failure.
8. **Key and device lifecycle:** protect keys in platform hardware where available; handle lost/reinstalled devices, backups, revocation, resets, compromised phones and offline revocation staleness. State whether funds are recoverable and the double-spend tradeoff.
9. **Limits, audit and privacy:** define per-transaction/offline exposure limits, expiry/sync deadlines and what metadata each peer/relay/backend sees. Avoid persistent device identifiers in BLE advertisements; rotate identifiers and minimize stored personal data.
10. **Platform reality:** iOS and Android BLE APIs/background behavior differ. Prototype the exact iPad-host/iPhone-client path on physical devices, including app foreground state, permission revocation, retries, MTU/payload limits and platform lifecycle before relying on a generic cross-platform transport abstraction.

For the current mocked flow, the most useful near-term scope is transaction-bound signed messages, explicit pending/confirmed UI, replay/idempotency, interruption recovery, and a documented relay threat model. True offline value requires a separate architecture decision and security review.
