# User-specific preferences for practice-question design

## Stable preferences observed

- The user wants **assistant-authored practice questions** to be clearly labeled as non-official.
- They dislike **obviously weak distractors** and will call this out explicitly.
- They also notice **answer-position patterning**; if the correct answer keeps landing in the same slot (for example, repeatedly in position 2), they want genuinely random placement instead.
- They want options to be **genuinely competitive**, not solvable by elimination of one absurd answer.
- When a question is weak, the preferred recovery is to **rewrite it immediately** rather than defend it.
- They often answer with a **choice plus commentary**; good handling acknowledges the commentary briefly.
- They prefer **clickable choices** when the format allows it.

## Practical implication

For this user, low-quality distractors are not a cosmetic flaw. They undermine the usefulness of the whole practice set. Treat distractor strength as a primary quality gate.
