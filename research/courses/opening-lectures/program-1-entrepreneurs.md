# Program 1 — Интернет-предприниматели с IT · opening lecture

**Audience:** internet entrepreneurs with an IT background — technically literate,
building their own products/businesses, able to read and steer code.

**Object of practice:** ship a real product / business increment.
**Base slot:** between careerfactory (non-developer builders) and khakhalev
(developer ICs); candidate job CJ6 (build end-to-end). The **IT background** sets
this apart from careerfactory's zero-experience builders — a partly open slot.

**The move it plants:** *AI as build spine, under control.*

## On screen

A real product increment — ship a working feature or a small MVP slice
end-to-end (landing + working form + deploy, or a real feature in an existing
product). Idea → running, directed — fast, but without vibe-coding into a mess
you can't defend.

## Top-level topics (as questions)

1. With your IT background, what can you now take on that you'd never have started alone — and what actually changed to make that real?
2. What separates *vibe-coding a demo* from *directing a build* you can still defend, maintain, and grow?
3. How much of what the AI produced do you need to understand to stay in control of your own product?
4. Where does a *real* product bite that a demo never does — auth, real data, payments, edge cases — and how do you keep AI useful there?
5. If you can ship the code this fast, what's the real bottleneck to a working business now — and is it even the code?

**Arc:** provocation (1) → mechanism / the distinction (2) → staying in control (3) → honest limit of real products (4) → landing that reframes the real bottleneck (5).

## Register note

Question 2 takes a deliberate stance in the contested "vibe coding" debate (see
`../positioning-notes.md`): neither reject nor blindly reclaim — *directing a
build you can defend* vs. a throwaway demo. Question 5 lifts the opener from
"how to code with AI" to "how to build a business," which is the program's real
promise.
