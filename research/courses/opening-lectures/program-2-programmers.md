# Program 2 — Программисты · opening lecture

**Audience:** working developers who want to level up their coding with AI —
*прокачать кодинг с AI*. Not "become an AI engineer"; adopt AI into the work
they already do well.

**Object of practice:** their own code / dev workflow.
**Base slot:** khakhalev (developer ICs); candidate jobs CJ2 / CJ5. The
"adopt-not-pivot" angle is the underserved gap flagged in both `../market-2026/`
reports.

**The move it plants:** *from typing to directing.*

## On screen

A real task from your actual week — a flaky test, a small feature, a refactor of
code you've been avoiding. Not a toy. Most people paste one file, say "fix it,"
get slop, and prove «сам напишу быстрее» right. The lecture does the opposite,
live.

## Top-level topics (as questions)

1. Experienced devs are often measurably *slower* with AI — «сам напишу быстрее» is frequently true. So what exactly are they doing wrong, and is it fixable?
2. What does the model need to know to be useful on *this* task — and why is almost none of it in the file you just pasted?
3. When you stop *writing* the code and start *directing* it, what's the new job — and what skill does it actually demand?
4. How do you spot confident slop fast enough that reviewing it doesn't cost more than writing it yourself would have?
5. Where is doing it by hand still the right call — and how do you know which mode you're in *before* you start?

**Arc:** provocation (1) → mechanism (2) → the shift (3) → judgment/verification (4) → honest limit (5).

## Register note

The «сам быстрее напишу» objection is answered by *doing*, not arguing — and the
honest "here it wouldn't help" moment stays in. That candor is the trust play.
