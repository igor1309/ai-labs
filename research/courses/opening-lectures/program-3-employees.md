# Program 3 — Служащие / обыватели · opening lecture

**Audience:** non-technical professionals and ordinary users who want to solve
routine tasks with AI — *решать рутинные задачи с AI*.

**Object of practice:** their own everyday work routine.
**Base slot:** aiforwork (non-developer operators automating their own routine,
tool-agnostic). Crowded — differentiation comes from **format + trust**, not
audience.

**The move it plants:** *grounding beats generic.*

## On screen

A recurring thing someone actually owes this week — a status report, a pile of
raw meeting notes → clean summary + action list, a messy spreadsheet. Do it the
lazy 30-second way first, look at the flat result together, then ground it in the
real materials and watch it become usable.

## Top-level topics (as questions)

1. Everyone's already tried this — so why does it still go generic the moment the task actually matters?
2. When the answer comes back bland, whose fault is it — the tool's, or what you handed it?
3. What would you tell a brand-new assistant that you never bother telling the AI — and why does closing that gap feel like cheating, or extra work?
4. For a real task, what actually counts as "your context" — what do you hand over, and what do you hold back?
5. How do you know the result is genuinely right, and not just fluent and sure of itself?

**Arc:** provocation (1) → mechanism (2) → the shift (3) → grounding made concrete (4) → judgment/verification (5).

## Register note

The viewer draws the conclusion from what's on screen — the difference between the
generic first pass and the grounded second pass makes the point without a lecture.
Question 4 also seeds the privacy/data-hygiene thread ("what do you hold back")
without turning the opener into a warnings session.
