# Positioning notes

Cross-source distillation from the two competitor extractions in this folder. Use as inputs to `decision-matrix.md`. No commitments, no chosen direction.

## Sources surveyed

- `khakhalev/` — AI Coding for experienced developers (Codex / Claude Code, RU).
- `aiforwork/` — AI agents for non-developer information workers (RU).

## Market map

The two surveyed courses sit on opposite halves of the AI-skills market and do not compete directly. The split is not "level" — it is **object of practice**:

| Axis | Khakhalev | AI For Work |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | mid/senior developers | non-developer information workers |
| Object of practice | shared codebase (Task Tracker) | participant's own real work routine |
| Tool framing | named tools (Codex, Claude Code; Cursor excluded) | tool-agnostic by design |
| Quality discipline | code review, security review, entropy | source verification before acting |
| Capstone | Plan & Act workflow on demo project | personal-routine automation, presented |
| Anti-positioning | refuses "vibe coding" | refuses both "vibe coding apps" *and* "no-code turnkey templates" |
| Pricing tiers (₽) | 30k / 50k / 80k | 34k / 54k / 69k (early bird) |
| Seat scarcity | top tier 15 seats | mid 30 / top 10 seats |

## Recurring positioning moves

Patterns both surveyed sellers use, regardless of audience:

1. **"System, not tricks."** Both reject prompt-template marketing and frame the product as a repeatable process.
2. **Anti-hype distancing.** Both explicitly refuse a nearby positioning the buyer already distrusts (vibe coding; low-code/no-code).
3. **Verification as a first-class step.** Both treat "how do I know the AI is right" as part of the skill, not an afterthought.
4. **Real-artifact spine.** Both anchor practice on something concrete (a codebase or a personal routine) rather than disconnected exercises.
5. **Tariff ladder with seat-capped top tier.** Self-serve / cohort / high-touch, with scarcity on the top tier.
6. **Short, named differentiator.** Khakhalev = "Plan & Act"; AI For Work = "task → input → result → verification." A nameable method packages the value.

## Empty positioning slots (white space)

Slots neither surveyed course occupies:

- **Engineering manager / tech lead audience.** Khakhalev sells to ICs; AI For Work sells to operators. Neither sells AI-coding rollout / evaluation playbooks for managers (matches CJ4 in the decision matrix).
- **English-language market.** Both surveyed offers are RU-only.
- **Senior developer judgment / failure post-mortems.** Khakhalev covers process; neither sells advanced "evaluate AI-generated code reliably" content for seniors specifically (matches CJ8).
- **Curated structured learning path** across roles, replacing fragmented self-study (matches CJ7). Both surveyed courses are role-specific.
- **Asynchronous, durable artifact-led format.** Both surveyed courses are cohort/live-anchored; nobody is selling a self-paced compounding library.
- **B2B / corporate licensing as a primary channel.** Khakhalev mentions employer payment support; neither leads with team rollout.

## Implications for our decision matrix

For each `Who / What / How / Differentiation / Business` axis in `decision-matrix.md`:

- **Who.** The two surveyed courses cover developer-IC and non-developer-operator. Picking either repeats an occupied slot. The unfilled audiences (engineering managers, senior judges, cross-role self-study) are stronger differentiation candidates if the underlying job (CJ4 / CJ7 / CJ8) is the one to investigate.
- **What.** "AI-assisted coding workflow" is the saturated topic in RU; "AI Coding discipline for managers" or "engineering judgment under AI" are not.
- **How.** The market norm is cohort-live with recordings. Asynchronous + durable artifact + community is open.
- **Differentiation.** A nameable method is table stakes — both surveyed sellers have one. The differentiator must come from the candidate-job push, not the method label.
- **Business.** The RU-only courses leave the EN market and the B2B-led channel open. Pricing in RU is anchored 30-80k ₽; an EN audience would reset that anchor.
