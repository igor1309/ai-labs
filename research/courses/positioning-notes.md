# Positioning notes

Cross-source distillation from the three competitor extractions in this folder. Use as inputs to `decision-matrix.md`. No commitments, no chosen direction.

## Sources surveyed

- `khakhalev/` — AI Coding for experienced developers (Codex / Claude Code, RU).
- `aiforwork/` — AI agents for non-developer information workers (RU).
- `careerfactory/` — Vibe-coding for non-developers who want to ship software products (RU).

## Market map

The three surveyed courses no longer split into two halves. The earlier two-source read ("developers vs non-developer operators") was an artifact of the sample. With a third source, the organizing axis is still **object of practice**, but it now has three values, and the "non-developer" audience splits into two distinct jobs:

| Axis | Khakhalev | AI For Work | CareerFactory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience | mid/senior developers | non-developer information workers | non-developers / aspiring founders / career-switchers (zero experience) |
| Object of practice | shared codebase (Task Tracker) | participant's own real work routine | participant's own shippable software product (Telegram bot) |
| Tool framing | named tools (Codex, Claude Code; Cursor excluded) | tool-agnostic by design | named real dev tools (Git, Docker, Cursor, VPS); no-code constructors excluded |
| Quality discipline | code review, security review, entropy | source verification before acting | "understand how it works" fundamentals + digital-security hygiene (keys, passwords) |
| Capstone | Plan & Act workflow on demo project | personal-routine automation, presented | deployed Telegram bot running 24/7 |
| Anti-positioning | refuses "vibe coding" | refuses both "vibe coding apps" *and* "no-code turnkey templates" | **reclaims** "vibe coding" ("healthy-person" version); refuses no-code constructors |
| Pricing tiers (₽) | 30k / 50k / 80k | 34k / 54k / 69k (early bird) | 19.9k / 29.9k / 44k / 154k |
| Scarcity | top tier 15 seats | mid 30 / top 10 seats | time/price (discount deadline, stream-start price lock); no seat cap |

The decisive new distinction is between the two non-developer offers: **AI For Work** teaches operators to automate *their own work routine* (no shipping, tool-agnostic), while **CareerFactory** teaches non-developers to *build and deploy a real software product* (real dev tools, VPS, 24/7). "Non-developer" is therefore not one slot — it is at least two jobs (automate my work vs ship my product).

## Recurring positioning moves

Patterns across the sample, with the third source forcing some earlier "both"-claims down to "some":

1. **"System, not tricks."** Khakhalev and AI For Work both reject prompt-template marketing and sell a repeatable process. CareerFactory leans more on *speed + empowerment + real tools* than on a disciplined system, so this is now a 2-of-3 move, not universal.
2. **Distancing from a distrusted neighbor — but the direction varies.** All three distance themselves from a nearby positioning the buyer distrusts, but not the same one and not the same way. Khakhalev *rejects* "vibe coding"; CareerFactory *reclaims* it as "vibe coding for a healthy person" and instead rejects no-code constructors; AI For Work rejects both vibe-coded apps and no-code templates. The term "vibe coding" is contested, not uniformly avoided.
3. **Verification as a first-class step.** Khakhalev (code/security review) and AI For Work (source verification) treat "how do I know the AI is right" as part of the skill. CareerFactory substitutes "understand the fundamentals" plus digital-security hygiene rather than output-verification discipline — so this is also 2-of-3.
4. **Real-artifact spine.** All three anchor practice on something concrete: a codebase, a personal routine, or a deployed product. This holds across the sample.
5. **Tariff ladder, but scarcity mechanism varies.** All three use a self-serve / cohort / high-touch ladder. Seat-capped scarcity is *not* universal: Khakhalev and AI For Work cap seats on top tiers; CareerFactory uses time/price scarcity (discount deadline, "lock in the price before the stream") instead.
6. **Short, named differentiator.** Khakhalev = "Plan & Act"; AI For Work = "task → input → result → verification"; CareerFactory = "vibe coding for a healthy person." A nameable hook is table stakes across all three.
7. **Authority anchor.** Each leans on a credibility source: Khakhalev on practitioner/teaching track record, CareerFactory on corporate pedigree (Ex-CPO Skyeng, "100x developer"). Personal-brand authority is a shared lever.

## Empty positioning slots (white space)

Slots none of the three surveyed courses occupies:

- **Engineering manager / tech lead audience.** All three sell to individuals (developer ICs, operators, or aspiring builders). None sells AI-coding rollout / evaluation playbooks for managers (matches CJ4 in the decision matrix).
- **English-language market.** All three surveyed offers are RU-only. The third source reinforces rather than weakens this gap.
- **Senior developer judgment / failure post-mortems.** Khakhalev covers process; none sells advanced "evaluate AI-generated code reliably" content for seniors specifically (matches CJ8).
- **Curated structured learning path** across roles, replacing fragmented self-study (matches CJ7). All three are role-specific.
- **Asynchronous, durable artifact-led format.** All three are cohort/live-anchored (CareerFactory adds a watch-only async *tier*, but the core product and pricing assume the live stream). Nobody sells a self-paced compounding library as the primary product.
- **B2B / corporate licensing as a primary channel.** Khakhalev mentions employer payment support; none leads with team rollout.

Slots now **occupied** that the earlier two-source map left blank:

- **Non-developer product-builder** (ship a real, deployed app without being a developer) is now taken by CareerFactory. With AI For Work also present, the non-developer audience is becoming crowded — which relatively raises the appeal of the still-empty manager and senior-judgment slots.

## Implications for our decision matrix

For each `Who / What / How / Differentiation / Business` axis in `decision-matrix.md`:

- **Who.** Three occupied slots now: developer-IC (Khakhalev), non-developer-operator (AI For Work), non-developer-builder (CareerFactory). Picking any of these repeats an occupied slot. The unfilled audiences (engineering managers, senior judges, cross-role self-study) are stronger differentiation candidates if the underlying job (CJ4 / CJ7 / CJ8) is the one to investigate.
- **What.** "AI-assisted coding workflow" is saturated in RU, and "build-your-own-product for non-devs" is now *also* occupied (CareerFactory). "AI Coding discipline for managers" and "engineering judgment under AI" remain open.
- **How.** Cohort-live with recordings is the norm across all three. Asynchronous + durable artifact + community remains open as a *primary* format, even though CareerFactory offers a watch-only tier.
- **Differentiation.** A nameable method is table stakes — all three have one. There is open room in the *stance toward "vibe coding"* itself: the term is contested (reject vs reclaim), so a deliberate position on it is a differentiation lever rather than a settled convention. The differentiator must still come from the candidate-job push, not the method label.
- **Business.** RU-only across all three leaves the EN market and the B2B-led channel open. The RU price anchor has widened to roughly 19.9k–154k ₽ (CareerFactory's 1:1 tier nearly doubles Khakhalev's top tier); an EN audience would reset that anchor entirely. Scarcity mechanism is a free variable (seats vs time/price).

## Open meta-questions raised by this survey

- Now that two of three courses target non-developers, is that market crowded enough to push us toward the still-empty manager (CJ4) or senior-judgment (CJ8) slots?
- "Vibe coding" is contested across the sample (rejected by one, reclaimed by another). Is taking an explicit stance on the term itself a viable differentiator, or noise?
- Does mirroring the surveyed cohort-live format save effort, or lock us into a slot already filled three times?
- Is the "system, not tricks" framing still a signal, or — now that one of three sells speed/empowerment instead — is the real divide between *discipline-led* and *empowerment-led* positioning?
- Which of the unfilled slots above is large enough to support a course, vs interesting but small?
- For an EN-market move, do we need a new survey of EN-market competitors before treating the slot as open?
