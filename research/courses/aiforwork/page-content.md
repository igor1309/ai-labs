## Positioning

The course is positioned as a practical AI-agents program for non-programmers whose work is information-heavy: marketing, research, sales, operations, finance, HR, analytics, and management. The framing is "delegate working routine to AI agents while keeping your expertise."

Core promise:

- Non-developers can run useful AI-agent workflows without writing code, working only through text, files, tables, and folders.
- Participants build their own automations during the course rather than receiving turnkey templates.
- The skills learned are transferable across changing tools because the course teaches a universal structure (task → input data → result → verification), not button-clicking on a specific product.

## Basic Course Facts

- Start date: 3 June 2026.

- Weekly load: about 30-40 minutes of homework, plus live sessions; optional extension for refinement.
- Format: online live sessions with retained recordings, community chat, homework on the higher tariffs, final project with presentation.
- Main practical project: each participant automates an actual work routine of their own and presents it at the end.
- Stack: text-first work with files, tables, folders, and websites — no API integration or coding required at the introductory level.
- AI tools covered: focus is on agent workflows accessible to non-developers (subscription, installation, payment, and first execution are walked through during onboarding).
- Access after completion: session recordings remain available.

## Stated Problems

The landing page frames the course around concrete information-work frustrations.

### Manual Data Transfer

Participants are described as moving information by hand "between dozens of tabs" across applications. Direct phrasing on the page: «Руками переносите информацию между _десятками вкладок_».

### Fear of Complex Files and Folders

Participants who lack technical confidence avoid working with structured files, complex folders, or batch processes that would otherwise save them significant time.

### Research Tasks That Eat the Day

Open-source research (job boards, market signals) and closed-source research (Telegram channels, chats) consume entire workdays when done manually.

### Decision Paralysis on Implementation

Even when participants understand AI conceptually, they cannot translate it into a workable routine and stall on "where to start."

### Tool / Subscription Overwhelm

Proliferation of AI products, plug-ins, and subscriptions creates noise rather than progress.

### Anxiety About Undetected AI Errors

Participants are wary that an agent will produce confident-looking but wrong results that they cannot catch — leading to either over-trust or over-rejection of AI output.

## Market Context

The page argues that AI skills are now appearing in job postings *outside* of software development, framing the course as career-relevant for non-engineering professionals: «навыки работы с ИИ приходят в вакансии за пределами разработки».

## Outcomes Promised After the Course

Participants should be able to:

- Establish secure agent-based workflows without programming.
- Transform unstructured data (raw tables, transcripts, page dumps) into clean, presentable reports.
- Conduct systematic open-source and closed-source research with agents.
- Use browser automation safely against forms, LinkedIn, dashboards, and web cabinets.
- Deploy at least one fully working automation against their personal work data by course end.
- Detect agent errors and verify sources before acting on agent output.

## Intended Post-Course Workflow

The course teaches a universal task structure that the page repeats as the unifying skill:

1. Define the task in plain language.
2. Provide the input data (files, tables, folders, links).
3. Receive the result from the agent.
4. Verify the result against sources or known-good cases.

The claim is that this loop transfers across tools, so changing models or platforms does not invalidate the participant's skill.

## Author Stance: "No Vibe-Coding"

The page explicitly distances itself from build-flashy-apps content: «Не будем вайбкодить приложения». The contrast is with low-code/no-code marketing — the course refuses to promise turnkey automations and instead frames the work as practical information-routine delegation.

## Program

The public program is organized as 6 weeks plus a guided onboarding week, anchored on each participant's real work data.

### Onboarding Week: Setup From Scratch

Focus:

- Subscriptions, installations, payment setup, first agent execution.

Participant takeaway:

- A working agent environment ready for hands-on use.

### Module 1: Data Processing

Focus:

- "From a messy table to a presentation."
- Structuring raw multi-source data (multiple tables, call transcripts, website content) into a clean clickable report with consistent visuals.

Participant takeaway:

- Repeatable pipeline for turning unstructured inputs into deliverables.

### Module 2: Open-Source Research

Focus:

- Collecting structured data from public sources (example: job boards) for hiring, sales, or market analysis.

Participant takeaway:

- A reusable research routine with verification steps.

### Module 3: Closed-Source Research

Focus:

- Processing Telegram channels, chats, and discussions for monitoring, summarization, and insight extraction.

Participant takeaway:

- A safe, repeatable workflow for high-volume conversational sources.

### Module 4: Browser Automation

Focus:

- Safe agent access to forms, LinkedIn, analytics dashboards, and web cabinets.

Participant takeaway:

- Confidence in delegating routine browser interaction without losing control or violating platform constraints.

### Final Project

Focus:

- Each participant automates one of their own real work routines and presents it.

Participant takeaway:

- At least one deployed, demonstrable automation against personal work data.
