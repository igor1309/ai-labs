**Notes.** The awareness/strategy layer is heavily **saturated** and commoditized at $0–$49 (Ng's courses are the default and near-free). The **premium, hands-on, cohort tier** for non-tech is thinner and dominated by **Section** (subscription, role-specific, high volume — pledged 50 AI courses) and **Every** (high-touch, $1.5–1.8k, personality-led). "Vibe coding for non-technical founders" is the fastest-rising sub-niche, riding Lovable ($300M ARR, 8M users by mid-2026) and Cursor; supply is currently cheap Udemy/Coursera with few premium guided offerings.

**Notes — the framing gap.** Almost every "tech" offering assumes the learner **wants to become an AI engineer** (build RAG/agents/LLM apps). Very little targets the actual "tech-not-yet-AI" persona: a competent, possibly **skeptical** developer who just wants to fold AI (Copilot/Cursor/Claude Code) into their *existing* workflow without a career pivot. The closest are the Udemy "AI for Developers with Copilot/Cursor" course and Dan Mason's 1-day Maven workshop — both light. **Tactical Agentic Coding** explicitly excludes beginners, confirming a hole at the "already skilled dev, new to AI, wants adoption not agent-building" entry point.

## 3. Format landscape

- **Self-paced lecture** (dominant, saturated): Coursera/DeepLearning.AI, Udemy, Scrimba (interactive but solo), Zero To Mastery. Cheap, low completion (industry 5–15%).
- **Cohort + live** (premium, growing): Section, Every, AI Makerspace, most Maven listings. Commands $750–$5,000; accountability/"forced completion" is the explicit pitch.
- **Hands-on / project-based**: AI Makerspace (ship an app), Udemy "real-world project" courses, vibe-coding bootcamps. Common at the tech end; weaker at the non-tech end (mostly concept lectures).
- **Socratic / two-person "learn by watching a dialogue"**: **Essentially nobody.** Searches surfaced only academic papers on AI-as-Socratic-tutor — **no commercial course uses an on-camera expert + skeptical-learner dialogue.** Clearest format-level white space. *(Verified absence from search; strong signal, not proof of zero.)*

## 4. Gaps & opportunities

1. **Format white space — the two-person Socratic recording.** No competitor does "expert + skeptical stand-in." Directly disarms the two hardest audiences: AI-anxious non-tech pros and AI-skeptical senior devs.
2. **"Tech-not-yet-AI" adoption angle is underserved.** Market conflates "learn AI" with "become an AI engineer." Helping *already-good* developers adopt AI into current work has thin, low-effort competition. High WTP, clear ROI story.
3. **Premium *guided* non-tech.** Field is barbelled: free/$49 awareness vs $750–$1,800 Section/Every. A mid-premium, genuinely hands-on "do it with me" product fits between.
4. **Non-tech + "build something real" (vibe coding).** Commercially hot (Lovable/Cursor), current supply is cheap. Room for higher-production, hand-holding version.
5. **Recorded + live hybrid.** Captures the accountability premium without the instructor-time ceiling of pure-live. Good margins.

## 5. Common complaints (from reviews/forums)

- **"Too basic / learned nothing."** Generic prompt-engineering courses teach only "basic one-liner prompts." *(Verified.)*
- **Outdated content** — students report wasting months on courses that lagged fast-moving tooling. *(Verified via search.)*
- **Billing/refund abuse** — Coursiv and Coursera drew charged-after-cancellation complaints. A clean refund policy (like Maven's) is a differentiator.
- **Low completion / no accountability** in self-paced (5–15%).
- **Lecture-only boredom / passivity** *(impression)* — drives demand for hands-on; supports the Socratic bet.
- **Overpriced for the depth** — the expert+skeptic format helps justify price by visibly modeling "getting your dumb questions answered."

## 6. Commercial signals (what's working)

- **Cohort sell-outs**: Lonely Octopus — last four cohorts sold out in <48 hrs to waitlist. Write Grow Sell — every cohort sold out in <7 days. *(Verified.)*
- **Every** runs repeat paid cohorts at $1.5–1.8k; **Section** scaled to membership + pledged 50 AI courses.
- **AI Makerspace** sustains a $4–5k, 10-week bootcamp with rolling cohorts + enterprise arm.
- **Vibe coding** is the demand wave: Lovable ~$300M ARR / 8M users mid-2026; "vibe coding" = Collins Word of the Year 2025. *(Verified.)*
- **Accountability/"forced completion"** is the repeated commercial pitch across winners.

**Bottom line:** Both planned courses hit real gaps. The **two-person Socratic recorded format is unoccupied**. The **"tech-not-yet-AI adoption" angle is genuinely underserved**. On non-tech, differentiate on *format* (skeptic-on-camera, hands-on "do it with me") and lean toward "build something real" over another "AI strategy" course. Recorded + live-webinar hybrid matches the accountability model driving sell-outs, at better margins than pure-live.
