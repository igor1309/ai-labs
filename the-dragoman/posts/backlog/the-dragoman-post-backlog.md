# Future Post Backlog

## 1. How I start an AI task

A post about the first move before asking anything: defining the shape of the work. What am I trying to get — draft, options, critique, research, decision support, code, naming, structure? The point is not a prompt template. The point is how I decide what kind of conversation I’m starting.

## 2. The moment I stop trusting the answer

A post about internal alarms during AI work: too smooth wording, generic confidence, missing constraints, suspicious symmetry, no friction with reality. Not as “AI is bad,” but as field notes on how my attention shifts from receiving output to inspecting the machinery behind it.

## 3. One task, three assistant roles

Take one real task and show how I split it: one assistant as explorer, one as critic, one as editor or builder. The post is about role separation as thinking hygiene: not because it is clever, but because mixed responsibilities make the conversation muddy.

## 4. Why I ask AI for options before answers

A post about using AI to expand the field before narrowing it. Options reveal assumptions, vocabulary, possible directions, and dead ends. The useful part is often not choosing one answer, but seeing the territory well enough to make a better human decision.

## 5. My current field-note format

Show the structure I might use after trying something with AI: context, attempt, what happened, what surprised me, what I would repeat, what I would change. This makes the channel’s method visible through an actual working format, not through manifesto language.
