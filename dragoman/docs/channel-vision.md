# The Dragoman — Channel Vision

> Founding document (2026-04-29). Voice and structure have since been refined in [`post-pipeline/voice-guide.md`](post-pipeline/voice-guide.md); treat that as authoritative when they diverge.

## Core idea

**The Dragoman** is a personal Telegram channel about navigating AI as a chaotic, fast-changing field of work, thought, tools, and creative practice.

The channel is not built around final answers.

It is built around discovery:

> try → observe → judge → compare → discard → keep what works.

## Channel promise

The promise is not perfect consistency of solutions.

The promise is consistency of method:

- direct experience;
- practical experiments;
- honest judgment;
- visible uncertainty;
- refusal of hype;
- translation between technical and non-technical worlds.

## Editorial center

The center of the channel is:

> AI as delegated work, creative tool, research partner, thinking amplifier, and unstable new professional terrain.

The channel can move across domains, but the recurring question stays stable:

> What actually works when AI meets real tasks, real constraints, and human judgment?

## Topic pillars

### 1. Delegating work to AI

How to give AI tasks that can be executed, reviewed, corrected, or abandoned.

Topics:

- briefing assistants;
- defining done;
- task decomposition;
- review loops;
- model choice;
- failure handling;
- human supervision;
- stopping criteria.

### 2. Practical assistant use

Personal and professional uses of AI assistants.

Topics:

- writing;
- research;
- learning;
- planning;
- document work;
- decision support;
- daily workflows;
- personal admin.

### 3. AI in software work

Technical practice as one important branch, not the whole identity.

Topics:

- coding agents;
- code review;
- agent orchestration;
- Claude Code / Codex-style workflows;
- reviewer/fixer loops;
- architecture as protection from FOMO;
- failure modes of automation.

### 4. Creative and narrative work

AI as collaborator in writing, story development, taste-testing, and creative exploration.

Topics:

- screenwriting;
- character work;
- story structure;
- visual concepts;
- idea generation;
- critique and revision;
- where AI helps and where it flattens taste.

### 5. Tools without tool-chasing

Tool discussion is allowed only when it serves practice.

Topics:

- what a tool is actually good for;
- what claims collapse under use;
- when a new feature changes a workflow;
- when a tool is just FOMO packaging;
- what to ignore.

### 6. Occasional reflection

Rare, sharp reflections on how AI changes work, judgment, delegation, creativity, and learning.

These posts should not become the main product.

## Voice

The voice should be:

- personal;
- exploratory;
- precise;
- skeptical without being cynical;
- practical without being shallow;
- technical when useful;
- readable for smart non-developers.

Avoid:

- guru tone;
- “AI will change everything” slogans;
- prompt-pack language;
- daily AI-news churn;
- fake certainty;
- motivational productivity prose;
- pretending every experiment generalizes.

## Post formats

### Field report

A practical post based on a real attempt.

Structure:

1. task;
2. setup;
3. what happened;
4. what worked;
5. what failed;
6. current conclusion.

### Tool judgment

A tool, model, or feature assessed through actual use.

Structure:

1. what it claims;
2. what I tried;
3. where it helped;
4. where it broke;
5. who should care.

### Workflow note

A reusable pattern for working with AI.

Examples:

- brief → execute → review → fix;
- cheap model drafts, stronger model reviews;
- AI as critic, not author;
- research assistant with source discipline.

### Translation post

A post that explains a technical AI/workflow concept for non-developers.

Examples:

- what an agent loop really means;
- why “memory” is not magic;
- why AI output needs review;
- why architecture matters even for non-coders.

### Reflection

A rarer essay-like post.

Examples:

- AI makes vague tasks more expensive;
- the new skill is supervision;
- judgment becomes more visible;
- taste survives automation better than routine execution.

## Suggested Telegram description

> Field notes from the AI Babylon: assistants, agents, workflows, writing, code, tools, failures, and practical discoveries.

## Suggested pinned post

Welcome to **The Dragoman**.

This is a personal channel about navigating AI as a strange new working territory.

Not AI news. Not magic prompts. Not guru doctrine.

I write about what I try, what works, what breaks, and what seems worth keeping — across assistants, agents, software work, writing, research, tools, and everyday workflows.

The role of a dragoman is not to own the map.

It is to move between worlds, translate what matters, and stay useful inside the chaos.

## Editorial rule of thumb

A post belongs in the channel if it answers at least one of these questions:

- What did I try with AI?
- What did I learn from using it?
- What workflow became clearer?
- What claim turned out to be false or exaggerated?
- What changed in how I work, write, think, or delegate?
- What would I repeat, avoid, or test next?

## Boundary rules

### In scope

- AI assistant workflows;
- AI-assisted work;
- coding agents;
- creative use of AI;
- practical experiments;
- tool judgment;
- personal operating loops;
- translation between technical and human domains.

### Out of scope

- daily AI news reposting;
- generic productivity hacks;
- prompt packs;
- untested tool hype;
- abstract futurism without contact with practice;
- developer-only implementation logs with no broader meaning.

## Long-term shape

The channel can evolve without breaking its identity.

Possible future branches:

- practical AI guides;
- paid notes or essays;
- workflow templates;
- small tools;
- curated experiments;
- project logs;
- interviews or conversations;
- companion GitHub repositories.

The stable core remains:

> A personal, practical, opinionated guide through AI as lived work.
