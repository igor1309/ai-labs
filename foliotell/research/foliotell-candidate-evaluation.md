## Sayfold

### Strengths

- Two syllables.
- Immediate pronunciation and likely spelling.
- **Say** clearly signals spoken action.
- **Fold** can suggest documents, folders, and stored content.
- Broad enough for formats beyond Markdown.
- Distinctive enough to function as a product brand.

### Concerns

- The document meaning is metaphorical.
- The product function may require an App Store subtitle or descriptor.
- Existing personal-handle usage may affect some social handles.

## FolioTell

### Strengths

- **Folio** clearly suggests a document collection.
- **Tell** supplies spoken delivery.
- Supports expansion beyond Markdown.
- Each syllable is clear and easy to pronounce.
- The construction communicates both content and action.

### Concerns

- Four syllables make it heavier than compact alternatives.
- The tone is slightly literary.
- The compound is understandable but not a conventional English phrase.

## Verbatone

### Strengths

- Compact three-syllable rhythm.
- **Verba** suggests words and verbal content.
- **Tone** signals voice and sound.
- Polished and format-independent.
- Strong memorability and brand character.

### Concerns

- Existing audio-software and audio-product usage creates search and brand friction.
- The exact product function is indirect.
- Availability requires deeper finalist-stage verification.

## Tellfolio

### Strengths

- Both roots are relevant to the product.
- Pronunciation is straightforward.
- Broad enough for a multi-format document library.
- More active and action-oriented than a document-first construction.

### Concerns

- The compound feels less natural than **FolioTell**.
- An educational e-portfolio software system has used **TELLfolio**.
- Four syllables reduce compactness.

## Tellmark

### Strengths

- Compact and easy to pronounce.
- **Tell** signals spoken action.
- **Mark** can suggest written marks or Markdown.
- Retains some Markdown relevance without explicit format lock-in.

### Concerns

- Spoken aloud, it can sound like the phrase **“tell Mark.”**
- **Mark** has broad meanings and provides a weaker document signal.
- Existing company and legal-service usage creates some search friction.

## Sonafolio

### Strengths

- **Folio** provides a strong document-library signal.
- **Sona** suggests sound while keeping the name brandable.
- Broad enough for future file formats.
- Has a polished, consumer-product tone.

### Concerns

- Five syllables make it the longest candidate.
- **Sona** may be pronounced in more than one way.
- Existing domain reputation and unrelated public usage require closer review.
- The speech function is less explicit than in names using **say** or **tell**.

## Comparative Observations

### Functional clarity

The clearest speech-action roots in this set are:

- **Say**
- **Tell**

The clearest document-library root is:

- **Folio**

The more indirect voice-related roots are:

- **Verba**
- **Tone**
- **Sona**

### Pronunciation

The most immediately pronounceable constructions are:

- **Sayfold**
- **FolioTell**
- **Tellfolio**
- **Tellmark**

The candidates with greater pronunciation or spoken-reference cost are:

- **Verbatone**
- **Sonafolio**

### Format flexibility

All six candidates can support expansion beyond Markdown.

**Tellmark** retains the strongest secondary association with Markdown, while the other names are more format-neutral.

### Descriptor dependence

Candidates likely to benefit from a permanent subtitle or descriptor:

- **Sayfold**
- **Verbatone**
- **Sonafolio**

Candidates with stronger built-in document semantics:

- **FolioTell**
- **Tellfolio**
