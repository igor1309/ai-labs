Method · Personal Working Approach

My Way

A judgment-driven method for software work. Coding is the execution step, not the primary thinking step. Software is a loop, not a line — and revisiting earlier layers when reality pushes back is part of the method, not a failure of it.

The core idea

Two phases share a single goal: keep product intent, execution, and recorded knowledge aligned as the work evolves. The pre-coding phase reduces ambiguity before implementation. The coding phase keeps execution honest against intent and turns the work into durable knowledge as it progresses.

Pre-coding phase

The front half of the loop. Reduces ambiguity before code is written by separating exploratory thinking from committed decisions, defining boundaries and contracts, and making implementation a constrained follow-through rather than discovery-by-coding.

Layer 1 · Parent

Product Intent

Defines who the work is for, the user-facing outcome, accepted user stories, acceptance signals, and non-goals. The user promise that downstream artifacts must preserve.

Layer 2 · Exploratory

Research

Frames the problem. Surfaces patterns, tradeoffs, constraints, risks, and assumptions that need validation. Avoids fake certainty — directed Q&A with AI assistants, not passive reading.

Pre-ADR

Solution-space thinking workspace before commitment. Compares options, captures the rough shape. Allowed to age out — its function is design digestion, not authority.

Coding phase

The back half of the same loop. Turns intent into executable form, executes through iterative verification, compresses what changed and why it matters, and preserves durable knowledge that outlives the immediate work.

  1. Executable spec turns intent into something concrete enough to execute, simulate, or test — behavior contracts, expected I/O examples, acceptance criteria as tests. Pressure-tests the spec and product intent before code is written.
  2. TDD cycle writes a failing test that expresses the missing behavior, makes it pass with the minimum change, then refactors and reconciles with the spec, plan, and product intent. Compilation errors are not RED — RED means the code compiles and the test fails on its assertion.
  3. Log records outcome and impact, not implementation detail. Git tracks what was done; the log tracks what changed for the user or the system. Concise, outcome-focused, user-facing.
  4. Artifact preserves durable knowledge that does not belong in code, ADRs, or specs — design decisions below ADR threshold, architectural outlines, postmortems, onboarding context. Produced when knowledge would otherwise be lost between sessions.

Scope variants

The full sequence is built for net-new architectural work. Two common cases follow lighter, alternative paths — escape hatches with explicit escalation rules back into the full sequence. That escalation is what keeps the lite paths honest.

minor feature story / Product Intent ref → optional research → mini-spec → short plan escalates on new boundary, protocol, or promise change
bug fix symptom → reproduction → root cause → fix strategy → regression test escalates to spec / pre-ADR on undefined behavior or violated invariant

Biases of the method

This approach is opinionated. It strongly favors several principles.

Decision before mechanics

Architecture, responsibility, and boundaries are settled before implementation details dominate the conversation.

Explicit boundaries

Ownership matters. Layer responsibility matters. Coupling is introduced deliberately, not by accident.

Progressive commitment

No premature formality, no indefinite vagueness. Movement from research to pre-ADR to ADR expresses gradual commitment when the situation calls for it.

Exploratory vs committed

Early documents are allowed to be imperfect or later outdated, provided they served exploration honestly. Committed documents should be sharper and more stable.

Execution, not architecture discovery

The later the stage, the less room for invention. Coding should not be the first place where the system is truly understood.

Iteration is not failure

Returning upward when implementation reality contradicts an earlier commitment is part of the method, not a sign that the process is broken.

Boundaries

My Way is intentionally narrow. Knowing what it does not do is part of the design.

My Way is

  • A judgment-driven personal working method
  • A loop that aligns intent, execution, and knowledge
  • An ordering of artifacts when the situation calls for them
  • Pressure on earlier layers when implementation reality pushes back
  • A separation of exploratory thinking from committed decisions
  • A commitment that artifacts are version-controlled alongside code

My Way is NOT

  • A claim of universal process correctness
  • A deterministic recipe or fixed checklist
  • Documentation for its own sake
  • Ceremony — ADRs, specs, and plans must earn their existence
  • One-shot paperwork — artifacts are living aids
  • A replacement for tests, ADRs, or product specs themselves

Review & AI assistants

AI assistants are used heavily throughout — not as a convenience layer, but as thinking partners. Different models are rotated deliberately across stages and review cycles to reduce blind spots inherent to any single model’s reasoning style.

Exploratory artifacts

Surface gaps

For research and pre-ADR, review surfaces gaps and marks them as explicit open questions. Imperfection is acceptable; honest exploration is required.

Committed artifacts

Adversarial by design

For ADR, spec, and implementation plan, review is adversarial. Encapsulated in dedicated review skills appropriate to the artifact type.

Product Intent

Story-first

Review checks that user stories remain first-class and that the artifact does not drift into vision, backlog, PRD, architecture, spec, or execution planning.
