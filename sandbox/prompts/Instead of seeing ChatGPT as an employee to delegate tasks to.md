# Content Creation

## intellectual sparring partner

Instead of seeing AI as an employee to delegate tasks to ("write this for me"), you should see it as an intellectual sparring partner or an "infinite intern."

Actionable Tip: Change your prompts.
Instead of: "Write a blog post about the benefits of remote work."
Try: "Here is my core belief: 'Fully remote work damages company culture, but hybrid models are the future.' Challenge this idea. What are the three strongest counterarguments? What data supports the opposing view? Who are the main thinkers who disagree with me?"
This shift forces the AI to help you think more deeply, anticipate criticism, and strengthen your argument, which is the foundation of thought leadership.

## Devil’s Advocate

using AI specifically to poke holes in your own thinking. This is the opposite of how most people use it (i.e., to find easy confirmation).

Actionable Tip: Make "playing devil's advocate" a standard part of your workflow. Before publishing anything, ask the AI:
"What are the weakest points in this argument?"
"What am I taking for granted or assuming my audience already knows?"
"Rephrase this concept as if you were trying to convince a complete skeptic."
This process makes your final content far more resilient and persuasive.
