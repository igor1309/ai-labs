Agister's run architecture

A run is what agister run leaves: a request that resolved, every step called on its runtime in a worktree Agister holds, and a record of who did what with what. Settings say what may run; this page says how it runs. The settings architecture is the half before it; the facts behind each part are in run-implementation.md.

From a command to a record

A run starts from a request: a workflow with its params, or a process on an agent, with --continue or --reuse naming an earlier run. Resolve turns it into definitions or a refusal. The composition plans every step, those in branch arms a run may not take included, and lists what this Agister does not run yet; a refusal of either kind ends the command before anything runs and before any record. agister check shares that list, so it refuses exactly what a run would.

What a workflow's items do

ItemRunsIts outputs
stepone call of its process on its agentthe call's output, checked against the process's schema
parallelits steps at once, or max at a time; one that fails does not stop the next; at least one must completea list, in declared order, of each step's status, class and output

repeatits body, then binds next over its state, then reads until; again until it holds or max passes have run; never reads until before the first pass{"state", "stopped", "passes"}: the last state, until or max, and how many passes ran

Items sit anywhere an item may: in the workflow's steps, an arm, a repeat's body; a parallel holds steps alone. A condition is pure: it reads params, inputs, a repeat's state and the outputs of items already ended, with equals, present, all, any, and, or and not. A number never equals a string, and params and inputs are strings. A repeat's body sees the state of the repeats around it, its own over theirs; a name next does not bind keeps its value.

A step in a repeat is named by its pass, <step>/<pass>, outer passes first where repeats nest (fix/2/1); the call, its record entry, its progress line, its events and reuse all use that name, and its plan is found by the name it is declared by.

A call

A call is one step executed. core/admit holds its worktree first, as a reader or a writer, checks a pinned revision and a clean worktree where the process asks, and keeps the call to its limits and the day's budget. core/call then makes turns on the runtime through its wrapper and the supervisor, which starts the program, keeps the ends of its output and ends its whole group:

  • a turn at capacity is made again, after 30 s, 1, 3 and 5 minutes, each telling the session to go on; then the call starts fresh on its agent's fallback, if it names one;
  • a turn that goes stale is followed by a recovery turn in its session, where the process allows a resume;
  • a call may continue an earlier step's session, by a binding; it goes on from where that session's latest call left the worktree;
  • each turn counts its own cost, claude-code's as reported, codex's priced through ccusage, against the process's limit and the daily budget.

At its end a call keeps the HEAD and content it left, and, where it pinned a revision, its changes as a patch.

What a run leaves

Each run is a folder under ~/.agister/runs, named by its id, its start's UTC time and six random hex characters:

FileHolds
record.jsonthe request, status and result; every call ended, as <run id>/<step>, with its process, agent, inputs, permission plan, environment, definition files by digest, HEAD and content, cost and result; written whole as each call ends, lost until the run ends
events.jsonla line per event as it happens: a step started, reused, a tool call, a stale turn, a turn at capacity, a fallback, a step ended; a branch's arm with each condition read, its values and whether it held; a repeat's pass with its state; why a repeat stopped
definitions/the bytes of every settings file a call was made from, by digest
patches/each pinned call's changes, by its name

Progress goes to stderr as it happens: a line per step started and ended, · <branch>: took <arm>, · <repeat>: ended pass <n> and · <repeat>: stopped <why> after <n> passes; the result, on stdout, is JSON. agister runs export copies a run out.

Running again

--reuse <run id> runs a request again and takes an earlier call of the same name, and pass, in place of making it, where its process, agent, inputs, definitions and environment are the same and the worktree is as that call left it. A call that continues a session, or whose session is continued, is made again. --continue <run id> goes on in the session of a one-step run's call.

What Agister does not do

Agister runs what settings define and records it. Committing, pushing, deciding what a review's findings mean and asking the owner stay with whoever calls it. It does not yet run a workflow inside a workflow, a branch or repeat inside a parallel, or a turn on pi; check and run refuse them alike.
