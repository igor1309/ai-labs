# Reinix Name Pool

## Purpose
This document preserves the naming terrain explored during the session that produced **Reinix**. It is not a decision brief. It is a reusable working artifact for future naming rounds.

## Working criteria used in the session
- control + orchestration, not “AI assistant”
- infra-serious tone
- somewhat legible, not literal
- avoids swarm / hive / copilot / maestro clichés
- avoids military or violent bluntness as primary tone
- avoids generic enterprise-manager naming
- works beyond coding-specific agents
- suitable for repo, package, CLI, and product identity
- 8–10 letters preferred when possible for distinctiveness

## Winner

### Reinix
**Status:** winner

**Why it worked**
- strong fit with governance, delegated authority, and orchestration
- not tied to coding-only use cases
- elegant but still serious
- avoids fluffy AI-product clichés
- distinctive enough in feel without becoming opaque

**Notes**
- visible search space is not perfectly clean
- Spanish morphology was discussed and judged not to be a real blocker for this use
- acceptable practical risk for OSS/project naming

## Rejection filters confirmed by the session
- too agentic / trendy
- too military / violent
- too orchestral / fluffy
- too abstract / empty
- too enterprise / boring
- too close to existing products or companies
- too software-generic in suffixing
- too person-name-like when product identity weakens

## Reusable semantic lanes for future stormings

### Governance / regency
Words and forms suggesting delegated authority, oversight, rule, stewardship.

### Harness / reins / control
Words suggesting disciplined direction over stronger executors.

### Routing / orchestration / flow
Words suggesting dispatch, vectoring, circulation of work.

### Corrective / adversarial / review loop
Words suggesting enforcement, scrutiny, feedback, controlled iteration.

### Feminine-elegant authority
A productive lane discovered late in the session: softer surface, but still strong governance semantics.

## Session takeaways
- the best results came from the **regency/governance** semantic core
- explicitly brute-force names were less effective than authority-with-distance
- the product wants a name of a **governing layer**, not a genius agent and not a workflow toy
- a slightly elegant finish improved the lane rather than weakening it
- feminine variants unexpectedly produced the strongest final result
